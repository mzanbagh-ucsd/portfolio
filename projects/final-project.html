How much longer do people live today—and where has that progress reached?

From 1960 to 2020, the median life expectancy across countries rose by 16.5 years. But that single global number hides very different journeys. Explore the pace of change across regions, then follow the countries behind it.

    Prototype notes

    What is built, and what comes next?

    What have I done so far?

    I prepared a country-year dataset from the World Bank life-expectancy indicator and joined it to regional classifications for 1960–2020. I built a first version of the explorable page with six views: the global distribution, regional trends, regional gaps, country trajectories, a gains ranking, and a country-year heatmap. Readers can change the years and region, add countries from the ranking or heatmap, and compare country trajectories. The page includes source notes and leaves missing observations blank instead of estimating them.

    What will be the most challenging part?

    The hardest part will be helping readers move between the global pattern and individual country paths without losing context. The heatmap includes more than 200 countries across 61 years, so labels and missing observations can become difficult to read. Regional medians can hide large differences within a region, while a ranking based on two endpoints can hide changes along the way. I plan to test the prototype with classmates, then use their feedback to refine the annotations, chart hierarchy, and controls.

    The takeaway

    Longer lives are a global story.
    Uneven progress is the story within it.

    Life expectancy summarizes many forces—healthcare, living conditions, conflict, and public policy. These charts show where the measure moved, and where gaps persisted. They do not explain every cause, but they make the differences easier to see and investigate.

    Source: World Bank indicator SP.DYN.LE00.IN, life expectancy at birth, total (years), 1960–2020. Country regions use World Bank regional classifications. Missing observations are left blank; they are not estimated.
