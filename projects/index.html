Project 2 Persuasive or Deceptive Visualization?

Mersad Zanbagh - DSC 209R

Proposition: Fossil fuel subsidies are still growing worldwide.

I used the IMF Fossil Fuel Subsidies dataset to argue both sides with the same 168-country sample and constant 2021 U.S. dollars. The first chart uses total subsidies, while the second uses explicit subsidies only.

Dataset: IMF Fossil Fuel Subsidies. Scores range from -2 (more deceptive) to +2 (more earnest).

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Matched sample (+2): I kept the same 168 countries across all years so missing data would not change the trend.
  • Broad definition (-1): Combining explicit and implicit subsidies creates a much larger total and strengthens the growth argument.
  • Area chart and endpoints (+1): The filled area emphasizes the size of the total, while the endpoint labels focus attention on the 40% increase and downplay the smaller dips.

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale:

  • Peak baseline (-1.5): Starting at the 2022 peak makes the decline look like the main trend.
  • Two-year window (-1.5): Showing only 2022 and 2024 hides the earlier variation and the fact that 2024 was still above 2015.
  • Slope chart without zero (-1): The steep downward line emphasizes direction more than absolute size.
  • Exact labels (+1.5): The chart still states the years, values, units, sample size, and explicit-only definition.

Final Reflection

Both charts are accurate, but they create different impressions because they use different definitions and time windows. The first chart combines explicit and implicit subsidies over ten years, making growth feel like the main story. The second uses explicit subsidies only and begins at the 2022 peak, making the recent decline feel more important.

The second chart is more deceptive because it removes earlier context and indexes the peak to 100. The numbers are correct, but a reader could assume there is a long-term decline. I now think ethical persuasion means being clear about definitions, baselines, and missing context. In a public report, I would place the full explicit-subsidy trend next to the two-point chart.
